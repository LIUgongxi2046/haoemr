# HaoEMR Code of Conduct

HaoEMR is a healthcare software community. Discussions and contributions must protect people, patient privacy, clinical safety, and the ability of participants to collaborate without harassment or intimidation.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and constructive when reviewing ideas and work.
- Critique technical or clinical claims with evidence, not personal attacks.
- Respect different experience levels, professions, languages, identities, and viewpoints.
- Minimize sensitive information and immediately redirect security or privacy reports to the private process in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).
- Correct mistakes transparently, especially when they affect clinical safety, data protection, licensing, or attribution.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized conduct, stalking, or deliberate intimidation.
- Publishing another person's private information, credentials, health information, or confidential institutional details.
- Knowingly presenting unsafe, fabricated, plagiarized, or unlicensed material as validated project work.
- Retaliation against a person who raises a good-faith safety, security, compliance, or conduct concern.
- Using project spaces for spam, deceptive promotion, or misleading claims of official HaoEMR approval.

## Scope and enforcement

This policy applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews, project events, and public representation of the project. Maintainers may edit or remove content, reject contributions, issue warnings, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban participants when necessary and proportionate.

For an ordinary conduct concern, contact the repository owner through <https://github.com/LIUgongxi2046/haoemr> and request a private channel if the report contains sensitive details. Security vulnerabilities must follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md). Do not publish patient information or confidential evidence in a public issue.

Maintainers should avoid conflicts of interest, preserve relevant evidence, hear affected participants when safe, and disclose an enforcement outcome at the minimum detail necessary to protect privacy.
